Some of the cool reading that the study leans on (I should add more but I got lazy). Also, reading the originals is worth the time and most definitely better than my summaries.

- [Iyengar, Sood and Lelkes, "Affect, Not Ideology" (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1093/poq/nfs038).
  The paper that put affective polarization on the map as a thing you can measure
  separately from what people believe.
- [Huddy, Mason and Aaroe, "Expressive Partisanship" (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055414000604).
  Party as an identity you feel, not a position you hold. Where the identity strength
  measure comes from.
- [Stanovich and West, "Reasoning independently of prior belief" (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.89.2.342).
  The actively open-minded thinking scale, and the argument for why it is worth
  measuring at all.
- [ANES](https://electionstudies.org/). Decades of feeling thermometers, which is
  what makes a single new thermometer reading mean anything.
- [David Eagleman, "Livewired" (2020)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livewired).
  Popular science on plasticity. It is where I got the "the brain is shaped by what
  it is fed" framing, and I hold it as loosely as that sentence suggests.
